# Pitch prep: the first buyer

2026-10-30. Prep mode from the pitch-coach skill, run on the target segment rather than a named prospect, so every number in brackets is an **assumption** to confirm in discovery. Swap in a real company before any call. Internal note: `files/` is excluded from the deployment.

## The prospect (archetype)
- **Who:** the People, HR or L&D lead at a 50 to 300 person company in AU, UK or SG. Professional services, healthcare admin, logistics or software, anywhere with steady hiring and written processes.
- **Current process:** onboarding and process training run as a live walkthrough by a manager, plus a slide deck or a 20-page PDF.
- **Suspected lost-money moment:** a senior person gives the same 45-minute walkthrough to every new starter, and people still get it wrong in week one because the PDF goes unread.
- **Decision maker:** the People lead recommends; a COO or founder signs off. [assumption]
- **Budget signals:** unknown. Many have an L&D or onboarding line. [assumption]
- **Lead source:** cold, by email or LinkedIn.

## One-line offer
I help growing teams stop re-explaining the same process to every new starter by turning it into a short video series they approve step by step, without anyone on their side learning video or animation.

Variant for professional services (the Explain job): I help firms stop losing the first meeting to explaining how they work, by turning their service or process into a short video clients can watch and forward.

## Discovery questions
1. Walk me through a new starter's first week. Where does someone sit down and explain things to them?
2. Who gives those walkthroughs today, and how long do they take?
3. How many people do you bring on in a typical month or quarter?
4. What do new people most often get wrong, or keep asking about, in the first month?
5. What happens when the process changes? How does everyone find out?
6. Have you tried recording it before (Loom, a webinar, a vendor)? What happened to it?
7. If this worked perfectly, what would be different for you or that manager?
8. Who else would need to see this before you could go ahead, and what would they need to feel confident?

Close the discovery by playing it back: "So [manager] spends about [X hours] a month on the same walkthrough, and people still ask [Y] in week two. Did I get that right?"

## What it costs them (assumptions, confirm all of it)
- [Walkthrough of 45 minutes] x [3 to 6 new starters a month] = [2 to 5 hours a month] of a senior person's time.
- At [$60 to $120 an hour] that is roughly [$1,500 to $7,000 a year] in time alone.
- **Honest read:** on time alone this only just covers a first project. The stronger cost is usually mistakes and slow ramp-up, or a process change that has to reach everyone at once. Find out which one hurts this prospect. If none of them does, it is a weak fit.

## Pitch flow
1. **Their problem in their words:** play back the walkthrough, how often it runs, and what still goes wrong.
2. **What it costs them:** their number, said back to them, not ours.
3. **What I'd make:** one short video of the explanation they repeat most, from the slides and notes they already have. They approve the script, then the look, before animation starts.
4. **A demo they can see:** the 0:47 process video and the training module on the Examples page, both made in the same system. Then the demo below.
5. **Options:** the table below.
6. **One next step with a date:** "Send me the deck for [process] by [day]. I'll send a scene plan by [day + 3], and we decide from there."

## Demo plan
- **Smallest proof:** a five-second loop of one step from their own public material (a careers page, "how we work", a published process), in their colours, through the existing `media/src` pipeline.
- **Effort cap:** about 2 hours, only for prospects who have replied or look like a strong fit. Anything bigger is a paid first project, not free work.
- **Label it:** an unsolicited concept made from public material. Never publish it or put it on the site without their written permission.

## Likely objections
1. **"We already use Loom / Synthesia / our team makes slides."**
   Acknowledge that Loom is fine for a one-off. Ask what happened the last time the process changed. Then explain the difference: a scripted, designed series where only the changed scenes get redone when the process moves.
2. **"You're one person, and in Vietnam."**
   Fair question. Final files and approved scripts are theirs. Review windows land in AU and SG business hours, and the UK morning overlaps Saigon's afternoon. State a reply time you can keep. *Decide before the first call: whether clients also get the project source files.*
3. **"Let's revisit next quarter" / "I need to get approval."**
   Ask what the approver will care about most. Offer a one-page summary written for that person, and a small paid first project with a success measure agreed in writing. Agree on a date to check back.

Also have a ready answer for data: no system access is needed, and you work from the approved material they choose to share (this is already on the site).

## Fit check
Worth pursuing if they hire steadily, the walkthrough is given by someone senior, and there is no in-house video person. Weak fit if they hire a few people a year, already have an L&D designer producing video, or only want a one-off "brand video".

## Inputs that would change this most
1. The real company, and which of the three jobs (Explain, Update, Teach) they need.
2. How often the explanation is repeated, and by whom.
3. Who signs off on the spend.

**Every cold email carries that footer:** who you are, where you are, and a working way to say no. Anyone who says no goes on a do-not-contact list (a single column in a spreadsheet is enough) and never gets another message. The AU, UK and SG rules all expect sender identification and an opt-out. This is not legal advice.

**After silence:** one useful nudge a week later (for example, a frame from a scene plan for a process like theirs), never "just checking in". After three with no answer, a short close-the-loop note.
