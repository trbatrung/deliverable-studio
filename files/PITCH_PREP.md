# Pitch prep: the credit plan

2026-10-30. Replaces the first-buyer prep (single piece, module, ongoing). The offer is now a monthly credit plan for smaller companies. Every price here is a **starting point to test with real buyers**, not a decided price. Internal note: `files/` is excluded from the deployment.

## The idea in one line
Anything your company needs to explain visually, made by one person on a monthly plan, paid in credits instead of per video.

## Why credits, not per video
- A per-video price gets compared with AI tools that cost about $30 a month. A monthly plan gets compared with hiring someone, which costs several thousand.
- What the buyer pays for is not the file. It is someone reading their notes, deciding what matters, writing it clearly, keeping it consistent and keeping it current, without anyone on their team learning a tool.
- Credits let one plan cover videos, loops, cut-downs and updates. The menu makes reuse cheap and starting from scratch more expensive, so clients choose the work the pipeline does fastest.
- Scope creep becomes "that's 2 more credits" instead of an argument.

**Internal rule, never shown to clients:** 1 credit is about 1 hour of Ethan's time. If an item keeps taking longer than its credits, change its credits.

## Who it's for
- **Company:** 5 to 50 staff in AU, UK or SG. The founder, ops lead or head of marketing decides alone, without procurement.
- **Signals:** hiring, launching something, raising, or selling something that takes a few minutes to explain. No in-house designer or video person.
- **Weak fit:** wants a one-off brand film, has an in-house video team, or wants unlimited everything.

## How payment works
No free trial: this is a service, not an app. Clients can always leave, but you are always paid before you work, so leaving never costs you money. Everything below goes in the quote before they pay.

| | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| **Deposit** | Half the first month up front ($250 / $750, or half the agreed figure for larger teams). Not refunded once work starts; it pays for the script and style frame. |
| **Balance** | The rest of month one, due when the first video's watermarked preview is ready. Clean files after payment. |
| **Month 2 onwards** | Invoiced on the 1st, paid in advance, work starts once paid. A month already paid is not refunded. |
| **Cancelling** | 30 days' notice in writing, effective at the end of the paid month. |
| **Loyalty** | 3 paid months in a row, the 4th month is free on the same plan. Leave early and there is simply no free month. Sold as a reward for staying, not a lock-in, since a minimum term can't realistically be enforced abroad. |

The script and style frame approvals are revision points, not exits: if they don't like the direction, revise within the included round. The terms page (`/terms`, "Monthly plans") says the same.

**In outreach, lead with the protection:** "Half up front, the rest after you've seen your first video."

**Capacity check:** about 130 working hours a month is about 130 credits. For example, 3 Standard + 3 Light is 129 credits and $6,000. Near capacity, stop taking larger teams or raise prices for new sign-ups.

## Discovery questions (by email, or a short call if they ask)
1. What do you find yourself explaining over and over, to customers, staff or investors?
2. Where does that explanation live today: a call, a deck, a PDF, a person?
3. What's coming up in the next three months that people will need to understand?
4. Have you tried making video yourselves or with an AI tool? What happened?
5. Who on your team would send me material and approve things?

## Likely objections
1. **"I can make this with an AI tool / Claude / Canva."**
   You can make a video. The work is deciding what to say, writing it so people get it the first time, and keeping every piece consistent and current. Offer the Light plan as a month to compare.
2. **"Can we try it first?"**
   No free trial, and say why plainly: it's a service with a person accountable for every video, not an app. What protects them instead: half up front, the rest only after they've seen their first video, and they can leave at the end of any paid month.
3. **"Credits are confusing."**
   Point at three lines of the menu that match what they just asked for, and add them up for them. If a buyer needs a calculator, the menu is too long.
4. **"Do you use AI?"**
   Yes, always say so plainly: "Yes, I use AI tools in production, under my direction. Your confidential figures and names stay out of them, I check every frame, and you own the final videos." Never deny it, and never sign a contract that bans AI unless they accept a carve-out (keep their confidential material out of tools that keep or learn from it). The narration voice is AI unless they choose a human one.
5. **"You're one person, and in Vietnam."**
   Final files and approved scripts are theirs. State a reply time you can keep. Review windows fit AU and SG business hours; the UK morning overlaps the Saigon afternoon.
6. **"Not now."**
   Ask what is coming up in the next quarter that people will need explained, and agree a date to check back.

## Demo plan
- **Smallest proof:** a five-second loop of one point from their own public material, in their colours, through the `media/src` pipeline.
- **Effort cap:** about 2 hours, only for prospects who replied or are a strong fit. Anything bigger is a paid first month.
- **Label it:** an unsolicited concept made from public material. Never publish it without written permission.

## Cold outreach
LinkedIn first, email second. Under 100 words before the footer, one ask, one link. Fill the bracket with something real from their site or LinkedIn.

**Customers (sales pitch):**
> **Subject:** Explaining [product] in under a minute
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I noticed [you launched X last month]. When a product takes a few minutes to explain, a short video usually does that job better than a call.
>
> I make them on a monthly plan, from your notes or deck: script, design and animation, with you approving each step. Here's a concept sales pitch in my style: [link to /work]
>
> Is there one thing prospects keep asking you to explain? Send it over and I'll reply with a rough scene plan. No call needed.
>
> Ethan

**Staff (onboarding and training):**
> **Subject:** The walkthrough every new starter gets
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I noticed [you're hiring for six roles this month]. When a team grows like that, someone usually ends up giving the same walkthrough to every new starter.
>
> I turn that kind of explanation into short videos on a monthly plan: you send the slides or notes, I write and produce, you approve each step. Here's a concept training module: [link to /work]
>
> Is there one process your team explains over and over? Send me the deck and I'll reply with a rough scene plan.
>
> Ethan

**Footer on every message:**
> Ethan Truong · Deliverable · Ho Chi Minh City · deliverable-project.vercel.app
> Not relevant? Reply "no" and I won't write again.

Anyone who says no goes on a do-not-contact list and never hears from you again. The AU, UK and SG rules expect sender details and a working opt-out. This is not legal advice.

**Sequence:** 3 touches over about two weeks. The second is something useful (a frame from a scene plan for a product like theirs), never "just checking in". The third is a short close-the-loop note.

**Volume:** a list of 50 companies, 10 new messages a day, everything tracked in one sheet (company, person, audience, date, touch, reply, outcome). Review after 100 messages: which audience replies, and what the objection is. If most say "too expensive", lower the price. If they say "not now", the price isn't the problem.

## Before the first send
1. **Time it.** Run one 60-second video and one loop through the pipeline, end to end, and check the credits hold.
2. **Only list what you can show.** Explainer, course module, sales pitch and pitch deck all have concept pieces now. Decide what file a deck client gets: PDF only, or an editable PowerPoint, Keynote or Google Slides file (the concept is built as HTML, so editable files would be rebuilt in their tool).
3. **Decide** the reply time you'll commit to, and whether clients get source files.
4. **Domain and email** are the parked final launch step. Outreach starts after them.
